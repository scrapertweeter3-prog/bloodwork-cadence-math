A blood panel is a sensor with a sampling rate

Bloodwork gets bought like an annual product, one panel a year because that is what the checklist says. A panel is closer to a sensor read: it samples the body at one point in time, and the sampling rate either matches what is being watched or it wastes money. Dosing logic is the whole game at HackedSelf, and the lab bill responds to dosing the same way a sauna schedule does.

The list price hides the unit. A fourteen-marker panel at $189 works out to $13.50 per biomarker read, and cadence multiplies that unit directly. Once a year the panel costs $189. Quarterly, the same fourteen markers run $756, and the three extra panels are $567 spent re-reading numbers that had months, not weeks, to move.

Frequency stops paying when the reading is smaller than the lab's own noise. Take an LDL result near 95 mg/dL on a test with an 8 percent lab CV. The readout carries a band of about 7.6 points, so a real six-point improvement over twelve months lands inside the band and the retest cannot confirm it. A second panel bought to see that change is $189 spent reading noise.

Cadence should track how fast the marker moves and how fast the intervention works. Markers under active steering get two reads a year, spaced far enough apart for real change to clear the noise band. Stable markers get the annual read. The two-read plan runs $378 a year and keeps every decision point a quarterly schedule genuinely uses, while dropping the two reads that land inside the noise.

Cost per usable readout makes the comparison blunt. Quarterly buys four reads, two of which carry information, so each usable read costs $378. The two-read plan spends the same $378 for its two reads, $189 each, half the price per decision you can act on. That is the arithmetic behind the bloodwork cadence math page: the panel is cheap, the retest schedule is what gets expensive.

Tally: $189 / 14 markers = $13.50 per read; annual $189, quarterly 4 x $189 = $756, extra 3 panels $567; 8% lab CV on 95 mg/dL = 7.6-point band, a 6-point change sits inside it; two-read plan $378/yr saves $378 = 50%; per usable read $378 (quarterly, 2 of 4 usable) vs $189 (two-read, 2 of 2).
